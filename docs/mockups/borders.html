Design review · TUI framing

finops v3 — Border treatments

These mockups compare bordered and borderless framing for the audit screens. Now that the TUI runs in the terminal's alternate screen, it owns the whole display — there is no scrollback above it and no shell prompt below it, so the question of where the application begins and ends is ours to answer rather than the terminal's.

The tradeoff under evaluation: a border states the app's bounds unambiguously, but it spends 2 of the 80 columns and roughly 1 of the 24 rows we are designing against — and that budget is already tight for the impact column.

A

Current: borderless

What ships today. Content runs edge to edge and the horizontal rules do all the structural work; the screen's boundary is wherever the terminal's own edge happens to be.

Outer border, title in the frame

C

Heavy: bordered sections

Header, content and footer each in their own box. Six of twenty-four rows go to border characters alone, the three frames compete for the eye rather than nesting, and the repeated corners read as clutter instead of structure. Rejected.

Tradeoffs

Weighing option B against option A.

What the border gains

  • Defines the app's bounds in the alternate screen — the frame, not the terminal window, is the edge.
  • Reads as a designed application rather than as command output that happens to be interactive.
  • Encloses the footer — the keybinding row is inside the app instead of floating above the shell prompt.
  • Absorbs the header and breadcrumb into the top rule, buying back most of the row the frame costs.
  • Section tees tie the detail pane to the same structure instead of introducing a second rule style.

What the border costs

  • 2 columns of 80 — the audit name field drops from 45 to 43 characters.
  • ~1 row of 24 once the bottom rule is paid for; the top rule earns its keep by carrying the title.
  • Broken rendering risk on fonts with poor box-drawing coverage, or terminals that mis-measure the glyph width.
  • Less room for wide tables — resource IDs and ARNs truncate two characters sooner in the results view.
  • Every full-width element must be padded exactly; an off-by-one anywhere shows up as a ragged right edge.

Proposal: adopt B. The two columns are recoverable by moving the header into the frame, and the alternate screen makes an undefined boundary the more expensive problem.
