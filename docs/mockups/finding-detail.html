Design review · Audit results

Showing a finding's detail without leaving the results screen

Today a finding's evidence lives on its own screen, reached with ↵ and left with esc. The question is whether that screen earns its place, or whether the detail belongs inside the results list.

Two things make the current split hard to defend: most of what the detail screen shows is already on the row you pressed ↵ on, and the screen disables ↑↓ — you cannot walk to the next finding without going back first.

why

How much of the detail screen is new information?

Every field on the detail screen, checked against the results row it drills into: ▸ vol-0f066ace215f73d98  ap-southeast-1  500 GB  gp3  $48.00

7 of 11 fields restate the row. Four carry new information: the evidence, the owner, the account, and the age. A whole screen and a navigation round-trip exist to deliver four facts — and the remediation command we designed but never built would be a fifth.

A

Expand in place, written as prose

Recommended

The selected row turns from ▸ to ▾ and three lines unfold beneath it: the arithmetic, the facts the columns cannot hold, and the command that fixes it. Neighbouring findings stay on screen.

B

Expand in place, keeping the labels

The same content, but retaining the detail screen's label column. More scannable, and it extends cleanly when a check has fields these do not — a stopped instance's uptime, a snapshot's source volume.

C

No expansion — a strip that follows the cursor

The detail sits in a fixed pane above the keybindings and updates as ↑↓ moves. No keypress, no list reflow, and it reuses the strip the catalogue screen already has.

The list never moves under you, and the detail is free — you get it by arrowing, not by asking. But the pane costs its 4 rows permanently, it caps evidence at two lines whatever the check needs, and it cannot show two findings' evidence side by side, which is exactly what "is 62 days unusual here?" requires.

scroll

What it looks like scrolled

A real audit returns tens or hundreds of findings, and the list does not scroll today — it is silently cut off at whatever fits. Adding bubbles/viewport belongs in the same change: expansion pushes rows past the fold, so the fold has to exist first. Position is stated twice, as a count in the header and as a thumb on the right edge.

The thumb spans the expanded block because the block is part of what is scrolled, not chrome floating over it. One consequence worth deciding: when a row is expanded and you press ↓ past its last line, does the cursor move to the next finding or into the block? Moving between findings is the common case, so the block should not be selectable.

vs

Trade-offs

A — prose block

  • 4 rows while open — the cheapest of the three.
  • Full command, untruncated — the one line worth copying stays whole.
  • Same gesture as the catalogue — the tool has one idea about drilling in.
  • Reads naturally because the evidence really is one sentence of arithmetic.

B — labelled block

  • Scannable — a label column beats reading a sentence to find the owner.
  • Extends cleanly to checks with fields these do not have.
  • 6 rows while open — two fewer findings visible at 80×24.
  • Truncates the command — 12 columns lost to labels.

C — following strip

  • Zero keystrokes — detail arrives by arrowing.
  • List never reflows under the cursor.
  • Costs 4 rows always — even when nobody is reading it.
  • Fixed height caps evidence at two lines regardless of the check.
  • No comparison — one finding's detail at a time, by construction.

Proposal: adopt A, and delete screenDetail. It costs the fewest rows, keeps the remediation command intact, and matches the expand gesture the catalogue already uses. Ship bubbles/viewport in the same change so the list scrolls before expansion starts pushing rows past the fold.

If the label column turns out to matter once more checks exist — stopped instances and orphaned snapshots have fields volumes do not — B is a change to one render function, not a change of architecture. That is the reason to start at A rather than B.
